Which debt cases become worth collecting when AI handles the routine work

Whitestar's CEO has said that Vexor allowed the company to work segments it had previously dropped because they were unprofitable, such as small balances where an agent costs more than the case recovers. This model estimates, segment by segment, which cases would become profitable for a servicer in Spain, and sets out a 90-day pilot to test the estimate.

Where the value comes from

What the model shows

For debtors who are willing and able to pay, AI changes the margin per case very little. The gain there is capacity, because the same team can handle a larger portfolio. Most of the additional margin comes from segments that are currently dropped, such as small balances, hardship cases that need a longer payment plan, and debtors who pay only once legal action begins.

Legal action in Spain

Debtors who are not facing legal action have little reason to respond to calls. Since 3 April 2025, Spanish creditors have had to show a good-faith attempt at negotiation (known as MASC) before filing most civil claims, including the monitorio. Vexor has said it plans to combine its legal and voice work in a single agent. If the record of its negotiation calls were accepted as MASC proof, the servicer could move to legal action sooner and at lower cost.

Limits of this model

This is not legal advice. The MASC point needs to be tested with a servicer's legal team. Spanish lawyers have warned that purely formal attempts may be rejected, and it is not yet clear whether call and SMS records count as reliable proof. If they do not, the benefit is limited to preparing the burofax letter automatically.

A 90-day pilot with agreed exit criteria

The Whitestar pilot was scoped around business outcomes and reached production in three months. Alen has written that the most difficult part was moving into production at scale. The plan below is one way to repeat that process with each new servicer, with exit criteria agreed before the pilot starts and measured against a control group.

Week 0: agree criteriaThe CFO, compliance lead and head of collections agree four targets for each segment.
Weeks 1–2: set upEach segment is split at random, 80% to Vexor and 20% kept with staff as a control group.
Weeks 3–10: runTeam leads review each case Vexor escalates. Scripts and limits are updated in a weekly review.
Week 12: decideSegments that meet the targets move to production. Segments that do not are stopped, while the rest continue.

Questions for the servicer in week one

  1. Which segments have you stopped working, and what figures led to that decision?
  2. Of your monitorio filings since April 2025, how many were delayed or rejected over MASC proof?
  3. Could I spend a day with a member of the legal team and time each step of a filing?
  4. What results would your CFO need to approve production?
  5. Which types of complaint take your team the most time to resolve?

What could change this conclusion

  • Segments may be dropped because of missing data, such as no address or no known assets, rather than cost. In that case, debtor research should come before collection.
  • Courts may not accept negotiation records as MASC proof. In that case, the legal benefit is smaller than the model assumes.
  • If the portfolio owner pays litigation costs, the servicer gains little from cheaper filings. The view selector above shows the result for each party.

Portfolio assumptions

Recovery and legal figures are a share of each case's balance, or of cases, per year. Changes update the chart and figures above.

How the model works

For the debt owner, revenue per case is the amount collected. For a servicer, it is the commission on that amount. The amount collected is the case balance multiplied by the recovery rate, plus the extra recovery on cases taken to court.

Without Vexor, each case costs six minutes of collector time per contact attempt, plus two hours of legal preparation at €35 an hour for cases taken to court. The debt owner also pays about €100 in court fees and €40 for a burofax to satisfy MASC.

With Vexor, each contact attempt costs the AI rate, and staff still handle the share of work set above. Legal preparation falls to 0.4 hours, in line with Vexor's published figure of legal work at about 20% of its previous cost. If the negotiation record is accepted as MASC proof, preparation falls to 0.25 hours and the MASC cost to €5. Vexor's fee is deducted from collections.

A segment is worked only if it makes a profit; otherwise it is dropped and earns nothing. Reviewing a blocked case takes 10 minutes of staff time. Staff numbers assume 1,600 working hours a year.
